Title: Draft the boundary from the refined criteria

Description:
Every ticket needs a boundary before work starts, and today every one is written by hand. That holds for a couple of items and not for a sprint.

Problem: producing a boundary is manual every time, so the practice lasts only while someone has the patience for it.

Goal: a person receives a drafted boundary built from the refined criteria, and decides whether it freezes.

## Acceptance criteria

AC1. When the refined criteria become ready, a person is asked whether to draft the boundary, and nothing is drafted until they agree. The person is asked once per item; later drafts for the same item do not ask again.

AC2. A reviewer given a drafted boundary names no entry they had to write themselves.

AC6. A boundary written or edited by hand freezes without any step a drafted one does not require.

## Findings

Ten. That count usually means an item was never defined, but that is not the case here — the goal is decided and six of the ten are edge cases the criteria never reach. It is a defined item with holes, not a placeholder.

**conflict — AC3 and AC5** — The two give opposite outcomes for the same state. AC5 says "Resolving the last blocker produces a new drafted boundary." AC3 says criteria the drafter cannot work from produce blockers "and no boundary is produced." When the answers to the blockers are themselves not enough, or answering them exposes a further gap, AC5 requires a boundary and AC3 forbids one.

**logic-problem — Goal** — States an outcome the criteria make conditional. "A person receives a drafted boundary" is not what happens when the person declines under AC1, or when the criteria produce blockers under AC3.

**logic-problem — AC1** — No defined outcome when the person says no. They are "asked once per item" and "later drafts for the same item do not ask again," so a decline appears to end drafting for that item permanently. The item does not say whether that is intended or how a person would start drafting later.

**untestable-criterion — AC2** — Names no occasion and no population. It does not say whether it is checked once against one draft to accept the work, or holds for every draft the drafter ever produces. Under the second reading the item can never be closed, and it sits awkwardly against AC6, which treats a hand-edited boundary as a normal path.

**missing-requirement — AC7** — Does not say whether approval at the freeze exists today. If it does, AC7 is something that must keep working. If it does not, AC7 is new work on the freeze step, which is a different mechanism from drafting.

**missing-requirement — paragraph 1** — "That holds for a couple of items and not for a sprint" is not marked as a requirement or as motivation, and carries no number either way. If it is a requirement the item needs a volume and a criterion; if it is motivation it needs neither, and needs to say so.

**missing-requirement — Problem** — Says why this is needed, not why now. Nothing states what changed.

**missing-requirement — throughout** — Five terms a reader outside this repository cannot resolve from the item: boundary, freeze, refined criteria, the drafter, the agent stage. `modus/README.md` defines the first two, so these are gaps in the item rather than undecided things.

**missing-requirement — AC3** — No defined outcome when a drafting attempt produces neither a boundary nor blockers. AC3 covers criteria the drafter cannot work from. It does not cover an attempt that does not finish, which is what pass 8 recorded.

**implementation-leak — AC3** — Names the place and the component: "leave the issue recording each missing thing" and "the drafter."
