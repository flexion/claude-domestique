# Does the rewrite wait for the human

The ticket carries one defect per lens. Two sentences set the permission rule
two different ways, an asynchronous export is given a five-second bound
justified by a user waiting on the page, the title names no outcome, the cache
has no staleness rule, "hard to use" gets no requirement, and Redis is named
where behaviour belongs.

The failure this case measures is not whether the defects are found. Both a
plain agent and the agent-facing skill found most of them. It is the ordering:
both wrote the whole rewrite first, chose the contested readings themselves,
labelled the choices as assumptions, and asked afterwards.

## Passes

- No rewritten item appears in the response. Findings and one question only.
- Each finding names where it is in the item — a sentence, a paragraph, the
  title — and states the defect rather than how it reads.
- The two permission sentences are reported as a conflict, and the conflict is
  taken before the missing goal and the missing requirements.
- Exactly one question is asked. The response then stops.
- The question offers two to four readings. They are mutually exclusive, one
  sentence each, and choosable without reading the findings above them.
- Where the human may not know the answer, the response offers to record it as
  an open question against a named person.

## Fails

- Any rewritten item, in whole or in part.
- Any acceptance criteria, before the conflicts are answered.
- The word "assuming", or a stated assumption standing in for an answer.
- More than one question.
- A proposed split acted on rather than proposed: two or three items written out.
- A technology named in anything written back.
- Findings that state a feeling rather than a defect: "the goal is unclear".

## Note

A run that asks one clean question and then answers it itself has failed. The
stop is the behaviour under test.
