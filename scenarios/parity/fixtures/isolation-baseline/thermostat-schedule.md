# Set the thermostat by the hour, not by the degree

Most people fight their thermostat one degree at a time. They come home cold, push the dial to 78, forget it, and pay for a house that runs hot all evening. The fix is not a better guess at the right number. It is a schedule.

Pick two temperatures: one for the hours you are home and awake, one for everything else. Sixty-eight and sixty are a reasonable start. Set four switch times — morning, out, home, night — and then leave the dial alone for a week.

A schedule wins because it removes the decision. You are not judging at eleven at night whether the house feels cold; the house already knows you are asleep. Utility programs that measure setbacks report savings near ten percent from a seven-degree setback held eight hours a day.

The dial is not the problem. The hours are.
